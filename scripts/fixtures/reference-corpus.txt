 SAMM SAMM self-amplifying momentum machine / home / launch / explore /twitter A new snowball model: volume-indexed buyback and burn, executed in flow-matched cycles that compound with every trade. snowballs burns into burns abstract A snowball is a coin whose fees are spent on itself. Every trade pays a creator fee; the fee buys the coin; the coin is burned; the smaller supply makes the next trade worth a little more of what remains. The idea is old and usually executed badly: fees claimed by hand, spent in lumps, at whatever hour someone remembered. This paper describes SAMM, a Self-Amplifying Momentum Machine that removes the hand. It claims continuously, spends only what it claimed, and sizes every buyback to the market that produced it, so that a busy hour is answered with a large buyback and a quiet one with a small one. Burn pressure becomes a function of volume rather than of anyone’s attention. The result compounds, the way a snowball does: every turn packs on a layer proportional to the ground it covered. 1 The snowball Every trade of a pump.fun coin pays a small creator fee, set aside in a vault that belongs to whoever created the coin. Most creators treat it as income. A snowball treats it as fuel. The fee is claimed, spent buying the same coin on the open market, and the tokens it buys are destroyed. That loop feeds itself. Buying adds demand; burning removes supply; both point the price the same way, and a higher price draws more trading, which pays more fees, which buys more coin. Nothing in it requires a promise. It requires only that the loop actually runs, every time, for as long as the coin trades. 2 How snowballs melt The loop is simple. Running it well is not. Nearly every buyback-and-burn coin fails in one of four ways: the lump Fees pile up for days, then one enormous buy prints a candle, gets sandwiched, and retraces by evening. the hand Someone has to remember to claim. They stop remembering, or they keep the fees instead. the wallet The burn script spends whatever the wallet holds, including money that was never fees. the void Buybacks land when nobody is trading, so each one moves the price more than it should and holds it less. Each of these is a timing problem or a custody problem. SAMM is built to remove both: it claims on a clock rather than on a whim, it can only spend what it claimed, and it times its buying to the market rather than to the calendar. 3 Momentum Let P be the pool of claimed fees not yet spent, and V 1h the coin’s trading volume over the last hour. Each pass, SAMM spends one slice of it: τ = min(P, max(τ min , ρ · V 1h )) ( 3.1 ) with ρ = 5 % and τ min = 0.01 SOL. The cap is the important part. No single buyback is ever larger than a twentieth of what the market itself traded in the previous hour, so SAMM never becomes the market. In a busy hour the cap is high and the pool empties quickly. In a quiet hour the cap falls to the floor, the pool drains slowly, and the unspent remainder waits for volume to come back. This is what makes the burn volume-indexed. The rate at which supply is destroyed tracks the rate at which the coin is used. SAMM is never early and never late; it simply follows the flow and takes its share. dS / dt ∝ − V ( 3.2 ) Supply S falls in proportion to volume. A coin that trades compounds. A coin that stops trading stops shrinking, but nothing it claimed is lost; it waits in the pool. 4 The machine Every coin launched here is created through pump.fun with its creator set to a fresh wallet that only the machine controls. A process called the keeper visits each one every 10 seconds and runs the same five steps: step what happens claim Collects the creator fees pump.fun has set aside for the coin. Only what arrives is ever spent. shave Tops the coin's own wallet back up to 0.01 SOL, so it can pay its own gas. size Sizes this pass's buyback from the pool of claimed fees, capped at 5% of the last hour's volume. buy Spends it on the coin: on the bonding curve, or on PumpSwap once it has graduated. burn Destroys exactly the tokens that buy delivered. Supply only moves one way. The accounting is deliberately narrow. SAMM measures a claim as the wallet’s balance after it minus the balance before it, and only that amount joins the pool. It never spends SOL it did not claim, and it burns only the tokens its own buy delivered, never anything the wallet already held. 5 What it will not do It will not sell. There is no path in SAMM that moves the coin in any direction but into a burn. It will not spend the gas reserve, and it will not spend fees faster than the market can absorb them. The keeper holds the wallet keys, so the rules are enforced by a process rather than by a contract. Everything it does is public: every claim, every buy and every burn is recorded with its transaction, and each snowball’s page shows them as they land. If the keeper stops, nothing is lost.
